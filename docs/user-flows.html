01

The role map

Seven working roles and the documents that pass between them. Read the arrows as responsibility changing hands — not as data moving.

Three properties of this map are worth stating plainly, because they are the ones that will be argued about.

  • Nothing enters except through the central store. No outlet takes a delivery directly from a vendor. It costs a little convenience and buys a single, trustworthy point of entry where batch, expiry, MRP and landed cost are captured once.
  • The counter never talks to procurement. A shortage at a counter becomes a request to the store; only the store's own shortfall becomes a purchase requisition. That keeps buying decisions in one place and stops five outlets independently ordering milk.
  • Finance approves but never originates. They see the purchase order for approval and the goods receipt for matching, and they reconcile the day. They cannot raise an indent, a request or a receipt.

A day in the building

The same seven roles laid against the clock. This is the rhythm the software has to fit into — not the other way round.

03

The eight roles

For each one: what they see the moment they sign in, the screens they move through, the loop they repeat all day, and — just as important — what the system will not let them do.

CO

Counter Operator

~12 users · coffee shop, restaurant, kiosk

Signs in and lands on Point of Sale
Point of Sale→Bills→ Stock on Hand→Stock Requests→Pick Tickets

Signs in with a four-digit PIN against a terminal already bound to an open shift — a full password at a queue-facing counter is shared within a week. The shift ties the operator, the terminal and the cash drawer together, so every bill has an owner.

Their day is one loop: take an order, tap or scan, tender, print. Everything else is interruption — an item goes dark, they raise a request, and later they collect a ticket.

Decisions they own

  • What goes on the bill and how it is tendered
  • Whether to hold a bill or take the next customer
  • Confirming a system-drafted stock request
  • Declaring cash at shift open and close

Hard stops. Cannot change a price, exceed the discount limit, cancel a bill alone, edit stock, or open any purchasing screen — the modules are absent from their sidebar, not merely disabled.

OM

Outlet Manager

3 users · one per selling outlet

Signs in and lands on Point of Sale, with approvals badged
Point of Sale→Approvals→ Price Lists→Stock on Hand→Bills→Reports

The supervisor on the floor. They can do everything a counter operator can, plus the things an operator is deliberately barred from: authorising a discount above the limit, cancelling a bill, and overriding an item's availability when a machine breaks rather than when stock runs out.

Their distinctive power is approving a lateral request — when another outlet asks for stock this outlet is holding, the decision is theirs, not the store's.

Decisions they own

  • Giving away their own stock to a peer outlet
  • Selling price within the approved band
  • Discounts, cancellations and complimentary items
  • Signing off the outlet's day close and cash variance

Hard stops. Cannot receive from a vendor, raise a purchase order, or set a price above a batch's printed MRP — that last one no role can do.

SK

Store Keeper

6 users · central and kitchen stores

Signs in and lands on Approvals
Approvals→Pick Tickets→ Goods Receipt→Batches & Expiry→ Requirement→Adjustments

The busiest role and the hub of the map. Deliveries land in the morning and become goods receipts with batch, manufacturing date, expiry, MRP and a quality decision. Requests arrive all day and become approvals, reservations and tickets. Stock leaves on a scan.

They land on the approval queue rather than a dashboard because a waiting request is a counter that cannot sell.

Decisions they own

  • How much of a request to grant, line by line
  • Accept, partially accept or reject a delivery at quality check
  • Which batch is issued when overriding the FEFO proposal
  • What the requirement list should actually say before it goes to procurement

Hard stops. Cannot change a purchase rate, approve their own stock adjustment, or sell anything. Receiving into a store they are not assigned to is not offered at all.

PI

Production In-charge

4 users · central kitchen

Signs in and lands on Production
Production→Recipes→ Stock on Hand→Stock Requests→Pick Tickets

Opens the day against a production plan. The screen shows each planned item with its recipe exploded into required quantities and a straight yes or no on whether the kitchen holds enough. Short of something, they raise a request to the store before the ovens are on.

Closing a batch is the single most consequential action in the system: raw materials leave stock, finished goods appear with a batch number and a best-before, and a unit cost is computed that every downstream price decision leans on.

Decisions they own

  • Batch size, and whether to open at all
  • Actual consumption against the recipe, with a reason for any variance
  • Recorded yield, rejects and production wastage
  • Pushing finished goods out to the outlets

Hard stops. Cannot set the selling price of what they produce, and cannot close a batch with an unexplained consumption variance.

PO

Purchase Officer

3 users · procurement

Signs in and lands on Requirement
Requirement→Vendors→ Purchase Orders→Goods Receipt→Reports

Never types a shopping list. The requirement arrives computed — stock netted against reorder point, unfulfilled requests, the production plan exploded through recipes, and trailing consumption — grouped by vendor with the arithmetic visible on every line.

Their work is sourcing: rate contract lookup, three quotes compared for anything off contract, vendor chosen and the choice justified in writing.

Decisions they own

  • Which vendor, and why, when a rate contract does not cover it
  • Order quantity after pack-size and minimum-order rounding
  • Delivery date and terms
  • Chasing short supply and short-closing an order

Hard stops. Cannot approve their own purchase order at any value, and cannot post a goods receipt — ordering and receiving are deliberately different people.

FA

Finance & Accounts

4 users

Signs in and lands on Reports
Reports→Purchase Orders→ Goods Receipt→Vendors→Bills→Price Lists

Two jobs. During the day they approve purchase orders that cross the value slab and clear three-way match exceptions where the vendor invoice, the order and the receipt disagree. At night they reconcile: counter tender against system tender, UPI and card settlement against the bank.

They also hold the second approval on any selling price that falls below computed cost, which is the control that stops non-MRP pricing drifting quietly into loss.

Decisions they own

  • Purchase order approval above ₹25,000
  • Releasing payment, or holding it on a mismatch
  • Accepting a below-cost price
  • Signing the day's reconciliation

Hard stops. Cannot raise an indent, a request, or a goods receipt. Approves and reconciles; never originates.

AD

Administrator

2 users · IT

Signs in and lands on Dashboard — all twenty modules visible
Dashboard→Items→ Locations→Users & Roles→Settings

Maintains the master data every other role depends on: items and their type flags, units and conversions, locations and their behaviour, tax codes, document numbering, the approval matrix and the period lock. Signs in with a password plus a one-time code, because these are the screens that can change what everyone else is allowed to do.

Decisions they own

  • Item master and the type flag that governs every screen
  • Who holds which role, and at which location
  • Approval thresholds and reservation expiry
  • Opening and closing the financial period

Hard stops. Deliberately excluded from transacting — cannot post a bill or approve a purchase order. Segregation of duties applies to the person who configures the rules more than to anyone else.

AU

Auditor

read only · management and internal audit

Signs in and lands on Dashboard
Dashboard→Reports→ Items→Stock on Hand→Batches & Expiry→Bills

Sees everything and changes nothing. Every dashboard, every report, the full audit trail including reversals and overrides, and export to Excel and PDF.

The role exists so that "let me just give them my login" never becomes the answer to an audit request — which is how transaction rights quietly spread through an organisation.

What they look for

  • Adjustments clustered around one person or one item
  • Overrides of the FEFO proposal without a good reason
  • Cash variances that repeat on the same shift
  • Wastage rising faster than sales

Hard stops. No transaction rights of any kind, anywhere, at any value.

DT

Dietitian

3 users · if patient diet is in scope — see UA-01 Q1

Signs in and lands on Ward census
Ward census→Diet plans→ Meal count→Consumption posting

Confirms meal counts per diet type against the ward census for each meal cycle, which deducts finished goods from the kitchen and posts either a diet charge to the patient's running bill or a cost to the ward cost centre.

Shown here in outline because whether this role lives in this module or in the separate HIMS diet module is still an open question.

Hard stops. Handles no cash and no stock directly — only confirms counts, which the system turns into movements.

Journey: a customer buys a coffee

Thirty seconds at a counter, and the shortest path in the system. Everything else in this document exists so that this one stays fast and stays true.

05

Journey: a counter runs dry

The loop that runs dozens of times a day and touches four roles. If only one flow in this document gets tested properly, make it this one.

06

Journey: one counter supplies another

Four in the afternoon, the store is shut, and the restaurant two floors down has what the coffee shop needs. This is the movement most likely to happen off the books unless the recorded path is the easy one.

Exchange is the same document, both ways at onceWhen two outlets swap — four litres of milk one way, two kilos of beans the other — both legs post in a single transaction. Two independent transfers would leave a window where one outlet has given stock away and not yet received anything, and if the second half is forgotten the books stay wrong with nobody chasing it.
07

Journey: production morning

Half past five, and the day's puffs and sandwiches do not exist yet. This is the flow that turns raw materials into something with a price.

08

Journey: buying what ran out

Four roles, an external party, and the longest chain in the system. Its distinguishing feature is that it starts with a computation, not with someone's opinion.

09

Journey: closing the day

The stage most systems skip, and the one that makes the whole ledger true. At close of business the counter must be emptied on screen the same way it is emptied physically.

Counter Operator21:30 · POS
Stops taking orders and opens shift close. The screen lists everything still on the counter, split by whether it survives the night. Class A traded goods → roll over  ·  Class B short-life → must be cleared
Counter Operator21:35 · Adjustments
Records wastage for unsold short-life stock with a reason code. Nine puffs at ₹17.79 go to the wastage account, not into tomorrow's opening stock. − 9 nos counter  →  + ₹160.11 wastage  ·  closing stock for that item is now zero
Counter Operator21:40 · POS
Declares cash in the drawer. The system compares it against the tender recorded across the shift and records the difference — a variance is explained, never absorbed. declared ₹18,420  ·  system ₹18,460  ·  variance −₹40 with a note
Outlet Manager21:45 · Approvals
Signs off the wastage and the cash variance. Above the auto-approve threshold both need a second pair of eyes, which is the whole point of having a supervisor on the floor.
System21:45
Locks the shift. From here a bill can no longer be cancelled — it can only be reversed by a credit note, which is a different document with its own approval. day locked against back-dated entry
Finance22:00 · Reports
Reconciles UPI and card settlement against the tender report, then runs the day reconciliation across every location. opening + receipts + production − issues − sales − consumption − wastage ± adjustments = closing
Why wastage is made deliberately visible rather than convenientIt would be easy to let unsold stock roll forward silently, and every counter would prefer it. But the daily wastage value per outlet is the single most useful signal you will get about whether the production plan matches real demand — a sandwich sell-through under 70% for four days running means the kitchen is making too many, and no other number tells you that.

11

Document lifecycles

Four documents carry the system. Each has a fixed set of states, and each state names who is holding it.

Stock request

Draft→Submitted→ Under review→Approved / Partial→ Ticket issued→Picked→ In transit→Received→Closed

Branches: Rejected at the decision, which issues no ticket at all. Part closed when a partial approval is fully collected but a back-order is still open. A reservation that is never collected expires and the stock returns to available.

Pick ticket

Issued→Picked — source debited→ In transit→Received — destination credited→Closed

Branches: Short received when the quantity scanned in is less than the quantity scanned out, which raises a discrepancy with an owner rather than balancing itself. Expired if never collected inside the reservation window.

Every screen watching a document updates itselfThe request → approval → issue → handover → receipt chain above runs on the server, and every open screen holds one live connection to it. The outlet manager does not refresh the approval queue to see a request the counter just raised, and the counter does not refresh to see the store keeper's ticket — the screen already changed by the time either of them looks.

Production batch

Planned→Open→ Consumption recorded→Closed — goods created→Labelled

A batch cannot close with an unexplained consumption or yield variance. Once closed it cannot be edited — a mistake is corrected by an adjustment against the batch, which keeps the variance history honest.

Purchase order

Draft→Pending approval→ Approved→Sent to vendor→ Partially received→Received→ Matched→Paid

Branches: Returned for revision with a reason, Short closed when the balance will never arrive, and Held when the vendor invoice disagrees with the order or the receipt.

One rule spans all fourNothing is ever deleted and no posted quantity is ever edited. A correction is a new document that reverses the old one and carries its identifier. That is what makes the day reconciliation in §09 provable rather than merely hoped for.
12

Where it breaks

Every handoff in this document is a place where a person can decide the software is slower than a phone call. These are the six most likely, what happens next, and what the design does about it.

The shortcutWhat goes wrongWhat the design does
Stock handed over without a ticket Source shows stock it no longer has, destination sells what the system says it does not hold. Both counters' figures are wrong and neither knows. The ticket, not the approval, is what opens the store room. A supervisor override exists for out-of-hours — at the store or the kitchen only — but it skips the collector's six-digit code on a ticket that already exists, not the ticket itself, and it writes one line to the audit trail: "Handed over — supervisor override".
Receiving scan skipped Quantity sits in transit forever. It belongs to nobody, so nobody chases it. In-transit items over a threshold appear on the store keeper's control screen as stuck, with the ticket, the time and the person who picked it.
Production entered after the fact Raw material consumption is guessed at the end of the shift, so variance is meaningless and the unit cost behind every price is fiction. Finished goods cannot exist without a closed batch. Nothing can be issued to an outlet that the kitchen never recorded making.
Wastage rolled forward instead of written off Closing stock is permanently overstated, yesterday's sandwiches never expire, and the production plan is never corrected. Shift close will not complete while short-life stock remains on the counter. The entry is required; only its reason is a choice.
FEFO proposal overridden habitually Old batches sit at the back until they expire, and the expiry write-off is blamed on demand rather than on picking. Every override carries a reason and lands on the issue document. A report ranks overrides by person, which makes the pattern visible without anyone having to accuse anyone.
One login shared across a counter The audit trail names a person who was not there. Cash variance cannot be attributed, so it stops being investigated. Counter access is a PIN bound to an open shift, so signing in is cheap enough that sharing has no benefit. The shift ties operator, terminal and drawer together.
The one that matters mostOf the six, the ticket is the load-bearing one. If stock moves between floors without a document, nothing downstream in this design works — not the availability calculation, not the reconciliation, not the wastage number, not the requirement run. Everything else in these three documents assumes that every physical movement has a scan behind it. That assumption is worth defending in training more than any feature is worth building.
