01

What we are building

One ledger from the vendor's lorry to the customer's bill, covering raw materials, in-house production and resale goods without letting any of them fall between the cracks.

The system tracks every edible and every packaging item that enters Royal Care Hospital through the kitchen and stores supply chain, follows it through storage, production and issue, and retires it in exactly one of four ways: sold at a counter, consumed by a department or patient, returned to the vendor, or wasted. Nothing is allowed to simply disappear — every unit that leaves stock has a document behind it, and every document has a person behind it.

Billing is not a bolted-on module. A bill line is a stock document that happens to also collect money, and it posts both in the same transaction: revenue and tax on one side, quantity and cost of goods on the other. That is what makes the closing-stock reconciliation in UA-24 possible at all.

In scope

Item & recipe masters, vendors and rate contracts, indent, approval, purchase order, goods receipt, quality rejection, multi-store stock with batch and expiry, issue to kitchen and outlets, in-house production, outlet stock and price control, counter billing with all tender types, patient and department consumption posting, wastage and day close, physical stock take, purchase and sales returns, GST output, dashboards and reports, audit trail.

Out of scope for this module

Pharmacy and medicine inventory, surgical consumables, linen, the asset register and patient clinical records — these are separate HIMS modules in the same platform and share the same login, masters and audit trail. Payroll is external; staff credit recovery is produced as a file, not a payroll posting. The existing HIS / patient-billing software is interfaced, not replaced.

Reading the colour codeEverywhere in this document, Class A · MRP means bought-in goods sold as they arrive, and Class B · In-house means goods the kitchen produces. Where a rule applies to only one of them, it carries the chip.
02

The two product classes

The MRP / non-MRP split you described is not a labelling difference. It changes costing, pricing authority, expiry maths, the number of stock ledgers involved and what a sale actually deducts.

 Class A — MRP / trade goodsClass B — In-house / non-MRP
ExamplesBottled juice, mineral water, tetra milk, biscuits, chips, sealed snacks, soft drinksVeg & egg puffs, sandwiches, tea and coffee, fresh juice, meals, patient diet trays, bakery
Where it comes fromBought finished from a distributorProduced in the kitchen from raw materials
Who sets the priceThe manufacturer. MRP is a statutory ceiling — the counter may sell at or below it, never aboveThe hospital. No statutory MRP, so the system must supply the discipline: a costed, approved, version-controlled price list
CostLanded cost = purchase rate + freight − scheme discount. Known at GRNRecipe cost = ∑ (ingredient qty × moving average rate) + overhead %. Recomputed at every production batch
Stock ledgers involvedOne. The purchase unit is the selling unitTwo. Raw materials in kg / L / nos, finished goods in nos / plate. Production is the bridge
Batch identityVendor's printed batch or lot numberSystem-generated production batch, e.g. PRD-20260825-03
ExpiryPrinted date, measured in months. FEFO issueShelf life in hours, set on the recipe. Best-before is stamped when the batch is produced
Stock goes up atGRN postingProduction entry — and raw material stock goes down in the same transaction
A sale deductsOne selling unit from the counter's stock of that MRP batchOne finished unit from the counter. The raw materials were already consumed at production time, not at sale time
When it does not sellNear-expiry → vendor return or approved markdownUnsold at day close → wastage. No vendor to send it back to
ReorderingReorder level and lead time on the item itselfReorder level lives on the raw materials. Finished goods are driven by a production plan against a par level

BarcodeVendor EAN, scanned as printedLabel the system prints: item, batch, best-before, price

There are actually four item types, not two

Classes A and B are the two things you sell. Underneath them the master needs two more types that never reach a customer directly, and giving them their own type is what keeps the reports honest — otherwise flour and finished puffs sit in the same stock value column and nobody can tell margin from consumption.

TypeSold?ExamplesBehaviour
RawNoMaida, oil, vegetables, milk, masala, LPGPurchased, stored, consumed by production. Valued at moving average
PackagingNoPaper cups, foil, covers, carry bags, tissueConsumed at production or at the counter. Optionally part of the recipe cost
TradedYesJuice, water, biscuitsBought sellable. MRP ceiling enforced
ManufacturedYesPuffs, sandwich, teaHas a recipe. Produced in batches. Hospital-set price
The MRP trap worth designing for nowThe same juice arrives at ₹20 MRP in August and ₹22 MRP in September. Those are not the same stock. If they are pooled, the counter will sell an old ₹20 bottle at ₹22, which is a legal metrology violation, or a new ₹22 bottle at ₹20 and lose the margin. Stock for Class A must therefore be held batch-wise and MRP-wise, and the POS must pick the price from the batch it is deducting, not from the item master.
03

Who logs in

Eleven named roles. The commercial scope covers 60 named users; the split below is our proposal for how those licences are spent, for you to confirm.

System Administrator

IT · 2 users

  • Users, roles, permissions, device registration
  • Item, vendor, store, UOM, tax and HSN masters
  • Document numbering series, financial period open/close
  • Full audit trail access

Cannot post a bill, approve a PO or change a posted stock figure.

Department Indentor

Kitchen, cafeteria, pantry, dietary · ~10 users

  • Raises daily, weekly and emergency indents
  • Sees par level and current stock for their own department only
  • Acknowledges receipt of issued goods

Cannot see other departments, costs or vendor rates.

Food & Beverage Manager / HOD

Approver · 2 users

  • Approves or returns indents
  • Sets the daily production plan and par levels
  • Approves recipes and Class B selling prices within limit
  • Approves wastage and stock adjustments
  • Sees every outlet and every cost

Cannot create a PO or receive goods.

Purchase Officer

Purchase department · 3 users

  • Vendor master and rate contracts
  • Quotation comparison
  • Converts approved indents into purchase orders
  • Follows up delivery and short supply

Cannot approve their own PO. Cannot post a GRN.

Purchase Manager / Finance Approver

Approver · 2 users

  • Approves POs by value slab
  • Clears 3-way match exceptions
  • Authorises debit and credit notes

Cannot edit the PO they are approving — only approve, or return with a reason.

Store Keeper

Central store, kitchen store · 6 users

  • GRN with batch, MRP, manufacturing and expiry date
  • Quality check, partial receipt, rejection to quarantine
  • Bin and rack allocation
  • Issue against approved indent, FEFO enforced
  • Stock take, cycle count, purchase return

Cannot change a rate or approve their own adjustment.

Production In-charge

Kitchen · 4 users

  • Opens and closes production batches
  • Records actual raw material consumption against recipe
  • Records yield, rejects and production wastage
  • Prints batch labels with best-before

Cannot set the selling price of what they produce.

Counter Operator

Cafeteria, canteen counters · ~12 users

  • Shift open with opening cash declaration
  • Billing, all tender types, held bills
  • Charge to patient bill or staff credit after lookup
  • Shift close with cash declaration and variance

Cannot change a price, exceed the discount limit, edit stock or cancel a bill alone.

Dietitian

Dietary · 3 users

  • Patient diet plans by diet type
  • Ward-wise meal count against census
  • Approves diet consumption posting

Cannot handle cash or stock.

Accounts / Finance

Finance · 4 users

  • Books vendor invoices against GRN, 3-way match
  • Vendor ledger, payments, credit notes
  • Day-end cash and tender reconciliation
  • GST registers, cost centre reports, Tally / Zoho export

Cannot create or approve an indent or PO.

Auditor / Management

Read-only · unlimited within licence

  • Every dashboard and every report
  • Full audit trail, including reversals and overrides
  • Export to Excel and PDF

No transaction rights of any kind, anywhere.

00

Masters and opening balance

System Administrator, Purchase Officer, F&B Manager · one time, then maintained

Nothing works until the item master carries the right type flag, because that flag decides which screens the item appears on. A raw material must never be sellable at a counter; a traded item must never appear in a recipe as an output.

  • Item master: code, name, type (raw / packaging / traded / manufactured), category, base UOM and purchase UOM with conversion, HSN, GST slab, shelf life, reorder level, par level
  • Recipes for every Class B item, with yield and overhead %
  • Vendors, rate contracts, lead times
  • Stores, outlets, bins, cost centres
  • Price list v1 for both classes
  • Opening stock upload, batch-wise, as at cut-over date

Inventory effect

Store — opening stock+ as uploaded
Opening stock value+ ₹ as uploaded
All other ledgersnil

Opening upload is a one-shot, permission-gated document. Once the period is opened for transactions it is locked; corrections after that are stock adjustments with approval.

01

Indent

Department Indentor · kitchen, cafeteria, pantry, dietary

Demand

The department states what it needs. The screen opens pre-filled from par level minus current department stock, so the common case is a review rather than a data-entry exercise. Three indent types — daily, weekly, emergency — each with its own approval path and its own colour in the queue.

Every line carries item, quantity, required-by date, usage purpose and cost centre. An indent for a Class B finished item is legitimate and becomes a production request, not a purchase request — the system routes it by item type.

Inventory effect

Physical stockno change
Indented quantity+ qty (soft)

An indent moves no stock. It creates visible demand so the same shortage is not indented twice by two departments on the same morning.

02

Approval

Supervisor, then F&B Manager / HOD by threshold

Control

The approver sees, on the same screen, what was asked for, what is already in stock, what is already on order and what this department consumed last week. Approving blind is the failure mode this stage exists to prevent.

Actions are approve, approve with modified quantity, or return for revision with a mandatory reason. A returned indent goes back to the raiser's queue, not into a void. Nothing is deleted; the revision history stays on the document.

Inventory effect

Physical stockno change
Indent status→ approved
Routed toPO or Production
03

Purchase order

Purchase Officer creates · Purchase Manager / Finance approves

ARaw

Approved indents for raw materials, packaging and Class A goods are pooled by vendor so one PO covers several departments' indents. Rate defaults from the live rate contract; any deviation is flagged and needs a reason before it can be saved.

Quotation comparison sits here for non-contract items: three quotes side by side, one selected, the reason recorded. The approved PO goes to the vendor as PDF, email or WhatsApp, and the document is locked from edit — changes after approval become an amendment with its own version number.

Inventory effect

Physical stockno change
On-order quantity+ qty
Indented quantity− qty
Financial commitment+ ₹ PO value

On-order shows in every stock enquiry. This is what stops the second PO for rice that already arrives tomorrow.

04

Goods receipt

Store Keeper · central store or kitchen store

ARaw

The first point where stock becomes real, and therefore the point that decides whether every downstream number is trustworthy. Receipt is against the PO, so ordered quantity, received quantity and rejected quantity are all on one line.

  • Batch or lot number, manufacturing date, expiry date — mandatory for anything with a shelf life
  • A MRP captured per batch. A different MRP creates a separate batch line, never a merge
  • Quality check: accept, partial accept, or reject to a quarantine bin that is not sellable and not issuable
  • Weight capture for loose goods, against the vendor's stated weight
  • Landed cost built up: rate, freight, scheme discount
  • Barcode label printed for anything without a usable vendor EAN

Inventory effect

Central store, new batch+ accepted qty
Quarantine bin+ rejected qty
On-order quantity− received qty
Stock value+ landed cost
Moving average raterecalculated
Vendor liability+ ₹ pending invoice
Hard blocks at GRNExpired or same-day-expiring batch cannot be received. Expiry earlier than manufacturing date is rejected. Receipt beyond PO quantity + 2% is blocked pending approval. Receiving into a store the user is not assigned to is not offered at all.
05

Stock at rest

Store Keeper · central, kitchen, outlet and quarantine stores

Not a transaction but a state, and the state that everything else reads. Stock is held at four levels of identity: store → bin → item → batch. Valuation is moving average by default; FEFO governs which batch leaves first, and the system proposes it rather than leaving it to memory.

Alerts run continuously rather than on a report anyone has to remember to open: below reorder level, below minimum, above maximum, near expiry at configurable horizons, expired, slow-moving, and negative-stock attempts.

Inventory effect

Balancessteady state
Ageing clockrunning
Alertsevaluated hourly

Negative stock is disallowed system-wide. If the shelf disagrees with the screen, the answer is a stock adjustment with approval, never a silent negative.

06

Issue

Store Keeper issues · receiving department acknowledges

Issue is always against an approved indent, and always to a named store or cost centre. The system proposes batches by FEFO; overriding the proposal requires a reason, which is how expiry discipline survives a busy morning.

Two different things share this screen, and the distinction matters for the P&L: a transfer moves stock and its cost to another store where it is still stock, while a consumption issue takes it out of stock entirely and charges a cost centre. Issuing rice to the kitchen store is the first; issuing cleaning consumables to housekeeping is the second.

Inventory effect — transfer

Central store− qty
Kitchen store / outlet+ qty
Total stock valueunchanged

Inventory effect — consumption

Issuing store− qty
Cost centre expense+ ₹ issue value
Total stock value− ₹ issue value
07

Production

Production In-charge · kitchen

B onlyAdded

The stage that turns raw materials into something with a price. It is missing from flow chart v1, and without it a puff can never legitimately exist in stock — it would have to be invented at the counter, which is exactly how kitchens lose track of flour.

A batch is opened against a recipe and a planned quantity. The system computes required ingredients, the in-charge confirms or corrects actual consumption, and on closing the batch the raw materials come out of the kitchen store and the finished goods go in, carrying a batch number, a best-before timestamp and a computed unit cost.

  • Yield variance: planned 120, actual 116, −3.3% with a reason code
  • Consumption variance: recipe said 6 kg maida, 6.4 kg was used — visible, and the trend is a report
  • Cost roll-up: material + overhead % = unit cost, which is what the price approval in stage 08 is checked against
  • Labels: one per unit or per tray, with item, batch and best-before

08

Outlet stock and pricing

Store Keeper transfers · F&B Manager prices

ABAdded

A counter can only sell what has been transferred to that counter. This is the control that makes counter-wise accountability possible at all — without it, every shortage belongs to everybody.

A Price is carried on the batch and capped at that batch's printed MRP. The POS reads the batch, not the item.

B Price comes from an approved price list version. The approval screen shows computed cost, proposed price and resulting margin side by side, so a price below cost is a deliberate decision and not an accident. Price lists are dated, so today's bill can always be re-explained six months later.

Inventory effect

FG store / central store− qty
Counter stock+ qty
Price list versionbound to batch
09

Sale and billing

Counter Operator · cafeteria, canteen, kiosk

ABAdded

One cart routinely holds both classes — a bottled juice and two puffs — and the bill has to handle them with different price sources, possibly different tax slabs, and identical speed. Scan an EAN for Class A, tap a tile for Class B.

Tender types

  • Cash, UPI, card — split tender allowed on one bill
  • Charge to patient bill after IP number lookup: no cash, posts to the running bill in HIS
  • Staff credit after employee lookup, against a monthly limit, recovered through payroll
  • Department consumption: no money, charges a cost centre
  • Complimentary, with reason and supervisor PIN

Bill is a GST tax invoice with HSN-wise summary. Cancellation within the shift restores stock and voids the bill; after shift close it becomes a credit note instead.

10

Consumption without a sale

Dietitian, Department Indentor · wards, pantry, staff canteen

Patient diet trays and departmental use are real stock movements with no cash attached, and if they are not captured the kitchen's consumption will never reconcile with its purchases.

Diet consumption is driven by ward census and the day's meal cycles: breakfast, lunch, snacks, dinner. The dietitian confirms counts per diet type, the system deducts the finished items and posts either a diet charge to the patient's running bill or a cost to the ward cost centre, depending on the answer to Q1.

Inventory effect

Kitchen / FG store− qty served
Patient bill or cost centre+ ₹ charge
Cash drawerno movement
11

Day close, wastage and reconciliation

Counter Operator, Production In-charge, Accounts

B criticalAdded

The stage that makes the whole ledger true, and the one most systems skip. At close of business the counter must be emptied on screen the same way it is emptied physically.

  • B Unsold short-shelf-life stock is written off as wastage with a reason code. Value goes to the wastage account, not into tomorrow's opening stock
  • A Unsold stock simply rolls over; near-expiry items are listed for markdown or vendor return
  • Counter cash declared and compared against system tender; variance recorded and explained
  • Shift closed; the day is locked against back-dated entry
  • Accounts reconciles UPI and card settlement against the tender report

Wastage is deliberately made visible rather than convenient. The daily wastage value per outlet is a management number, and over a month it is the single most useful signal about whether the production plan matches demand.

12

Reporting, settlement and control

Accounts, F&B Manager, Management, Auditor

Reports

  • Stock summary and stock ledger, store-wise and batch-wise
  • Purchase register, GRN register, vendor performance and short supply
  • Consumption by department and cost centre
  • Production report with yield and consumption variance B
  • Sales by counter, shift, operator, item and tender
  • Margin report: selling price against computed cost
  • Wastage and expiry loss
  • Near expiry, expired, slow and dead stock
  • GST output register and HSN summary, purchase register, Tally / Zoho CSV
  • Staff credit recovery statement for payroll

Controls

  • Role-based access, right down to which store a user may touch
  • Full audit trail: who, what, before value, after value, when, from which terminal
  • Every override and approval stored on the document
  • 3-way match — PO, GRN, vendor invoice — before payment is released
  • Periodic physical stock take with variance report and approval
  • Cycle count on high-value and fast-moving items
  • Period lock after day close and month close
  • No deletion anywhere; reversal documents only

10

Your decisions

Ten answers we need before the SRS can be written. Most change scope; two change the architecture.

Is patient diet inside this module?

Diet planning and ward meal counts could sit here, or in the separate HIMS diet module with only the stock deduction crossing over. Changes scope by roughly one module.

Do the counters need to keep billing when the network is down?

Offline-capable billing with sync-on-reconnect is a materially different build from a browser-only POS. Architectural.

How many outlets, counters and stores, and where?

The quotation assumes five stock locations. Every counter beyond that is a licence, a terminal and a training slot.

Does the kitchen actually work to standard recipes?

If yes, the system back-flushes automatically and only asks about exceptions. If cooking is by judgement, actual consumption must be entered at every batch. Architectural — see stage 07.

What is the GST position on food served to inpatients versus sold to visitors?

These are commonly treated differently, and the treatment decides whether one counter can issue both a tax invoice and an exempt document. Please have your CA confirm the position in writing — we will build to that, not to an assumption.

Is staff canteen credit recovered through payroll?

If yes, we produce a monthly recovery file. Tell us the format your payroll accepts and whether a credit limit per employee applies.

What POS hardware exists today?

Thermal printer models, cash drawer, barcode scanners, weighing scales for loose goods, label printer. Integration effort depends entirely on the answer.

Do you want made-to-order handling with a kitchen order ticket?

Relevant if items such as sandwiches are prepared after the order rather than in advance batches. It adds an order queue screen for the kitchen.

Which billing or HIS software must we post patient charges into?

Name, version, and whether it exposes an API or only a database. This determines whether posting is real time or a file at day end.

What is the cut-over date and who owns opening stock?

We need item masters and batch-wise opening stock in clean spreadsheets, and one named person per department to sign that the counted opening figure is correct.
