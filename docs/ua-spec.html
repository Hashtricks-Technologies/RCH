01

What we are building

One ledger from the vendor's lorry to the customer's bill, covering raw materials, in-house production and resale goods without letting any of them fall between the cracks.

The system tracks every edible and every packaging item that enters Royal Care Hospital through the kitchen and stores supply chain, follows it through storage, production and issue, and retires it in exactly one of four ways: sold at a counter, consumed by a department or patient, returned to the vendor, or wasted. Nothing is allowed to simply disappear — every unit that leaves stock has a document behind it, and every document has a person behind it.

Billing is not a bolted-on module. A bill line is a stock document that happens to also collect money, and it posts both in the same transaction: revenue and tax on one side, quantity and cost of goods on the other. That is what makes the closing-stock reconciliation in UA-24 possible at all.

In scope

Item & recipe masters, vendors and rate contracts, indent, approval, purchase order, goods receipt, quality rejection, multi-store stock with batch and expiry, issue to kitchen and outlets, in-house production, outlet stock and price control, counter billing with all tender types, patient and department consumption posting, wastage and day close, physical stock take, purchase and sales returns, GST output, dashboards and reports, audit trail.

Out of scope for this module

Pharmacy and medicine inventory, surgical consumables, linen, the asset register and patient clinical records — these are separate HIMS modules in the same platform and share the same login, masters and audit trail. Payroll is external; staff credit recovery is produced as a file, not a payroll posting. The existing HIS / patient-billing software is interfaced, not replaced.

Reading the colour codeEverywhere in this document, Class A · MRP means bought-in goods sold as they arrive, and Class B · In-house means goods the kitchen produces. Where a rule applies to only one of them, it carries the chip.
02

The two product classes

The MRP / non-MRP split you described is not a labelling difference. It changes costing, pricing authority, expiry maths, the number of stock ledgers involved and what a sale actually deducts.

 Class A — MRP / trade goodsClass B — In-house / non-MRP
ExamplesBottled juice, mineral water, tetra milk, biscuits, chips, sealed snacks, soft drinksVeg & egg puffs, sandwiches, tea and coffee, fresh juice, meals, patient diet trays, bakery

Who sets the priceThe manufacturer. MRP is a statutory ceiling — the counter may sell at or below it, never aboveThe hospital. No statutory MRP, so the system must supply the discipline: a costed, approved, version-controlled price list

There are actually four item types, not two

Classes A and B are the two things you sell. Underneath them the master needs two more types that never reach a customer directly, and giving them their own type is what keeps the reports honest — otherwise flour and finished puffs sit in the same stock value column and nobody can tell margin from consumption.

TypeSold?ExamplesBehaviour
RawNoMaida, oil, vegetables, milk, masala, LPGPurchased, stored, consumed by production. Valued at moving average
PackagingNoPaper cups, foil, covers, carry bags, tissueConsumed at production or at the counter. Optionally part of the recipe cost
TradedYesJuice, water, biscuitsBought sellable. MRP ceiling enforced
ManufacturedYesPuffs, sandwich, teaHas a recipe. Produced in batches. Hospital-set price
The MRP trap worth designing for nowThe same juice arrives at ₹20 MRP in August and ₹22 MRP in September. Those are not the same stock. If they are pooled, the counter will sell an old ₹20 bottle at ₹22, which is a legal metrology violation, or a new ₹22 bottle at ₹20 and lose the margin. Stock for Class A must therefore be held batch-wise and MRP-wise, and the POS must pick the price from the batch it is deducting, not from the item master.
03

Who logs in

Five roles, exactly as built — RoleSchema in packages/contract/src/schemas/common.ts — each with the modules spec §8.3 lists for it. There is no sixth role: what earlier drafts split into Indentor / Manager / Purchase Officer / Purchase Manager / Accounts / Administrator / Auditor collapses onto these five, because that is what the module list below actually needs, not a headcount plan.

Counter Operator

Coffee Shop, Restaurant, Snack Kiosk · 2 seeded users

  • Point of Sale, all tender types, MTO recipe deduction
  • Turns an item on/off at their own counter
  • Raises and cancels stock requests; receives a ticket at their own outlet
  • Asks a peer shop for stock, and answers a peer's ask, at their own outlet
  • Raises a support ticket; withdraws a shop-to-shop transfer they raised

Cannot approve a request, issue a ticket, price anything, or open any purchasing screen — those modules are absent from their sidebar.

Outlet Manager

All three outlets · 1 seeded user

  • Approves, trims or rejects a stock request — never past what the store can still promise
  • Sets prices on both lists, adds and removes menu items
  • Turns an item on/off at any outlet
  • Sees a shop-to-shop ask, but does not decide it
  • Raises a support ticket

Cannot receive from a vendor, raise a purchase order, or price above a batch's printed MRP — that ceiling has no override, for anyone.

Store Keeper

Central Store · 1 seeded user

  • Issues a ticket against an approved request; hands it over on the collector's OTP or the labelled override
  • Central stock, requisitions to procurement, rate contracts
  • Adds a product directly to the master
  • Answers a shop's request for something not on the master
  • Reads the stock ledger and the credit report; raises a support ticket

Cannot change a purchase rate, sell anything, or approve their own stock movement.

Kitchen In-charge

Central Kitchen · 1 seeded user

  • Walks the production board (New → Accepted → In kitchen → Ready)
  • Makes a batch — ingredients off, units on, one transaction
  • Dispatches a production order and distributes stock, all-or-nothing
  • Kitchen stock; hands a ticket over from the kitchen
  • Reads the stock ledger; raises a support ticket

Cannot set the selling price of what they produce, or close a batch that would take an ingredient negative.

Procurement Officer

Not tied to one outlet · 1 seeded user

  • Approves or declines a requisition, whole or line by line
  • Vendors, purchase orders end to end, goods receipt with tolerance and quarantine
  • Answers a shop's request for a new product by sourcing it
  • Raises a support ticket

Cannot raise a requisition, approve their own purchase order at any value, or receive without a delivery to check against.

How sign-in actually works

StepWhat actually happens
CredentialsEmployee number and password — nothing else on the sign-in screen. No store picker: the account's location is fixed at creation (pnpm --filter @rch/api users create --loc …) and travels in the token, not chosen on the way in. No counter PIN and no shift binding — a session is the person, not the terminal
Password storageArgon2id (memory 64 MiB, iterations 3, parallelism 1), never anything reversible
The tokenAn EdDSA-signed JWT, 15-minute expiry, carrying the employee id, role and location; a rotating refresh cookie (30 days) renews it without a second password prompt, and presenting an already-used refresh token revokes the whole session family
First sign-inAn account created or reset by the CLI carries must_change_password, which routes straight to a change-password step before anything else in the app is reachable — not an admin screen, because there is no admin screen; there is a CLI (spec §8.5)
Supervisor overrideNot a PIN, not a second sign-in — handing over a ticket with the OTP field left blank is the override, open to the store keeper and the kitchen in-charge only, and recorded on the ticket's own trail as “Handed over — supervisor override”
Every module a role does not have is absent from its sidebar and refused with 404 on a direct link — the same “this does not exist for you” answer a URL for a page that was never built would get, not a permissions error naming what you may not do. Location decides which rows within a module you may act on, and answers 403 when it refuses.

00

Masters and opening balance

Loaded once by the seed CLI, maintained by whichever role's screen owns the row

The item master carries a type — Raw, Packaging, MRP (traded) or FG/MTO (made) — and that flag is what keeps a raw material off the till and a traded item out of a recipe as an output. Five locations plus a sixth, quarantine, that never sells and is never offered on any write that names a location.

  • Items: code, name (unique, case-insensitive), unit, type, HSN, GST slab, reorder level, cost, printed MRP where one applies, shelf-life hours for a made item
  • Recipes for every made item, with an overhead percentage
  • Locations, each with a type and, for a sellable one, a price list
  • Two price lists (A, B) and each outlet's own menu — which items it lists for sale
  • Users, created and maintained by a CLI (spec §8.5) — there is no user-administration screen
  • Opening stock, written as opening moves on the ledger, the same ledger every later movement posts to

Inventory effect

Every location — opening stock+ as seeded
All other ledgersnil

There is no period-lock concept in this build. A correction after go-live is a reversing move on the ledger, never an edit — stock_moves refuses UPDATE and DELETE at the database level.

01

Requisition, purchase order, goods receipt

Store Keeper raises · Procurement Officer decides, orders and receives

One raiser, one decider, no department-by-department indent. The store keeper reviews what the central store needs and sends a requisition; the procurement officer approves it whole, trims it line by line, or declines it with a reason — free-to-promise is not consulted here, because what the central store is holding has nothing to do with what a vendor can supply.

An approved-but-unordered line sits on the procurement list — a query over open requisitions, not a stored pool. The buyer picks lines onto a draft purchase order, priced from a live rate contract when one covers the item, sent to the vendor with no further approval gate (§04's own note explains why). Receiving books what passed inspection onto the central store's shelf and what did not into quarantine, both in the same transaction, so the books can never show one without the other.

Inventory effect — one receipt

Central store+ accepted qty
Quarantine+ rejected qty
Requisition line's claimordered_qty moves

A delivery more than 2% over what was ordered is refused whole — not partially booked. Batch, manufacture date, expiry and the vendor's printed MRP are captured per receipt line for the record; an already-expired batch is refused outright, as is one whose expiry precedes its manufacture date.

What did not carry through from this document's original designStock is not held batch-wise or MRP-wise on the shelf — each item carries one flat balance per location and one printed MRP on the master, not a batch-priced one. FEFO issue, quotation comparison, purchase returns and debit notes, and 3-way match against a vendor invoice were none of them built; §09 records each and why.
02

Stock at rest, including quarantine

Every location, read the same way

A state, not a transaction — the one every other stage reads and writes. stock_balances is a cache, one row per location and item, derived from an append-only ledger of every move that has ever happened and rebuildable from it at any time (db:rebuild-balances). Negative stock is refused system-wide by a lock-then-check on every write that moves stock down.

quarantine is real, exactly as this document's own topology below already has it: a sixth, Store-typed location that holds whatever a goods receipt's quality check turned away. It never sells, is never offered on an issue or transfer screen, and nothing in this release moves stock back out of it — a correction is a hand-written adjustment move, not a document the system raises.

Inventory effect

Balancessteady state
Below-reorder, near-expiryread at dashboard-open, not scheduled

A balance row's presence is itself a fact: it means the location carries that line, so a shelf holding zero still shows a row, and a shelf that has never carried the item shows a dash. Alerts are computed when a screen is opened, not evaluated hourly in the background.

03

Issue by ticket

Store Keeper or Kitchen In-charge issues · the collecting outlet receives

Approval authorises; the scan moves. A counter or the kitchen raises a stock request; the outlet manager approves it, in whole or trimmed — never past what the store can still promise once every open ticket and other approval is netted off. Approving writes a reservation, not a movement. The store keeper turns an approved request into a ticket, which carries a six-digit code minted at random and known only to the collecting location while the ticket is open.

The collector reads the code aloud; the issuing desk types it to hand over, which is the instant stock actually leaves. A wrong code is refused; an absent collector is the labelled supervisor override, open to the store and the kitchen only and written to the ticket's own trail. Between handover and receipt the stock is in transit, owned by neither location. The same ticket mechanism carries a shop-to-shop transfer and one shop's direct ask to a peer — and any ticket nobody has collected can be withdrawn, which puts the hold and the document behind it back exactly where they stood.

Approvalreservation only, no move
Source, at handover− qty
Destination, at receipt+ qty
Total stock valueunchanged
04

Production

Kitchen In-charge

B only

The kitchen board walks New → Accepted → In kitchen → Ready, one press at a time; dispatch is its own action from any open stage, because the kitchen sends an order out the moment it is ready, whatever the board still says. A batch consumes a recipe's ingredients — measured against what is free to promise, not what is merely on the shelf, so stock another ticket is holding cannot be baked with — and books the finished yield, both in one transaction. Ingredients go against what was started; only the yielded units reach the rack.

A short cover refuses the whole batch and names the ingredient; nothing moves. Every finished unit carries a best-before stamped from the recipe's shelf-life hours, and its unit cost is computed from the recipe plus overhead at read time — never stored, so a later change to an ingredient's price re-prices every past batch's display rather than leaving it wrong.

05

Sale and billing

Counter Operator · Coffee Shop, Restaurant, Snack Kiosk

AB

One cart, both classes: a bottled item deducts by the unit at its capped price; a made-to-order item deducts its recipe from the counter's own ingredient stock, not a finished unit, because the raw materials were already consumed at production time. Deducting them again here would double-count them.

Tender types

  • Cash, UPI, card
  • Charge to patient bill or staff credit or department — each validated against a payer roster the server holds, never a name typed at the till

Staff credit carries a hard monthly ceiling per person, hospital-wide, not per till. GST is derived from the inclusive price the counter reads. Two counters can never both sell the last unit: the row lock that guarantees it is taken before either bill posts.

Inventory effect — one bill

Counter stock, traded item− qty sold
Counter ingredient stock, MTO item− recipe qty
Revenue and output GSTposted together
Bill numberCF/<n>

No raw material moves here for a made item — it left stock at production, not at sale.

What was built, what was declined, and why

This document described thirteen stages and eleven roles before anything was built. Six stages and five roles are what shipped. Nine of the original twenty-four UA scenarios tested a feature that was deliberately not built, and this section is where each of those nine goes on the record, by name, rather than quietly disappearing when the table above was rewritten.
